Buyer's guide to Grease & Oil Remover

How emulsification works

Grease and oil do not mix with water, so rinsing alone moves them around rather than removing them. An emulsifying remover breaks oily deposits into tiny droplets surrounded by surfactant, so they can be suspended in water and rinsed away. This makes cleaning faster and leaves parts ready for inspection or reassembly.

Where grease builds up

Gearboxes, chains, conveyors, bearings housings, compressor bodies and machine bases all collect grease and dirt over time. That build-up hides cracks and leaks, attracts dust and can become a fire risk near hot surfaces. Regular removal keeps equipment visible, safe and easier to maintain.

Soak, brush or spray

Small parts can be soaked in a diluted solution, larger items brushed or sprayed. Allowing a short dwell time lets the product penetrate before rinsing. Warm water often speeds up removal on heavy deposits, where the material allows it.

Handling the run-off

Oily rinse water should be collected and handled according to site rules rather than sent straight to storm drains. Seamens can advise on practical handling for your workshop.

Protecting cleaned parts

Freshly degreased steel can rust quickly in humid weather. Dry parts promptly and apply lubricant or protective coating before storage or reassembly.

Choosing between degreaser grades

For very heavy carbon and oil, the heavy duty degreaser may be more suitable; for routine mechanical parts, the grease and oil remover is often enough. Seamens helps you choose.

Saving labour time
