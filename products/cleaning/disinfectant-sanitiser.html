Liquid or foam

How Disinfectant / Sanitiser works

Disinfectants and sanitisers kill or reduce microorganisms on surfaces. Their performance is proven in laboratory tests that measure the reduction in bacteria under set conditions and contact times.

In practice, surfaces are cleaned first to remove soil, then the sanitiser is applied as liquid or foam and left for its contact time before rinsing or drying as directed.

Buyer's guide to Disinfectant / Sanitiser

Clean first, then disinfect

Disinfectants work on clean surfaces. Dirt, grease and food residues shield microorganisms and consume active ingredient. A two-step routine, cleaning then disinfecting, gives reliable hygiene.

Contact time is essential

Every disinfectant needs time in contact with the surface. Wiping it off too soon reduces its effect. Follow the stated contact time.

Choosing the right product for the area

Food-contact surfaces, medical areas, washrooms and clean rooms each have different needs. Food areas need products and rinsing practices suited to food contact, clean rooms need low-residue products, and washrooms need broad action against common bacteria. Seamens recommends the product and routine for each area of your site.

Training staff

Disinfection only works when staff dilute correctly, cover the surface and wait for the contact time. Short training with clear wall charts makes the routine consistent.

Storage and shelf life

Store disinfectants as labelled, away from heat and direct sunlight, and use diluted solutions within the stated time.

Supply

Regular supply keeps hygiene steady.

What good hygiene looks like

Visibly clean surfaces, good swab and ATP results, and complete cleaning logs that satisfy food safety and hygiene auditors.
