Buyer's guide to Drain & Pipe Unclogger

What blocks industrial drains

Drains in kitchens, canteens, washrooms and production areas block with a mixture of grease, soap scum, food waste, fibre and scale. Grease cools and hardens inside pipes, catching other debris until flow slows and finally stops. Chemical clearing breaks down the organic part of the blockage so water can flow again.

When chemical clearing makes sense

Chemical unclogging suits slow or partly blocked drains where the cause is organic build-up. Hard objects, collapsed pipes or root intrusion need mechanical methods. If a drain blocks repeatedly in the same place, a camera inspection may show a deeper problem.

Safe use

Drain chemicals are strong and must be handled with care. Follow the label, wear gloves and eye protection, never mix with other drain products and keep the area ventilated.

Prevention is cheaper

Grease traps, strainers and staff awareness keep most solids out of drains. Regular maintenance doses can keep lines flowing between cleanings.

Pipe materials

Pipe materials such as PVC, cast iron and steel are considered before use. Ask Seamens for your system.

Reducing downtime

A blocked drain in a production area or kitchen can stop work. Having the right product on site shortens disruption.

Understanding grease build-up

In canteens and food areas, warm greasy water flows into drains and cools, leaving fat on pipe walls. Over weeks, these layers grow and trap food particles and soap. Regular treatment and grease trap cleaning prevent the gradual narrowing that leads to blockages.

After clearing

Flush the line well with water after treatment and inspect any traps or chambers for remaining solids.
