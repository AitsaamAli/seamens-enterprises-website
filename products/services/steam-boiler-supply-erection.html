Choosing the right boiler

Fire-tube boilers suit most process steam loads in textile, food and general industry and are simpler to operate. Water-tube boilers handle higher pressures and larger outputs. Thermic fluid heaters give high process temperatures without high steam pressure, which suits drying and finishing.

The right choice depends on steam demand, pressure, fuel and the space available, so sizing starts from your process load.

From supply to commissioning

Erection covers placing the boiler, the boiler room pipework and connection to your steam and feed systems. Seamens also supplies the Water Safe boiler chemicals, so water treatment is planned from day one.

How Steam Boiler Supply & Erection works

Boiler selection starts from the process: how much steam per hour, at what pressure, with what load swings and which fuel. Fire-tube boilers suit most industrial loads up to 25 ton/hr; water-tube boilers handle higher pressure; thermic fluid heaters deliver high temperature without high pressure.

Erection then covers foundations, placement, boiler room piping, feed and blowdown systems and safety valves, followed by hydro test, commissioning and handover with a water treatment programme in place.

Where Steam Boiler Supply & Erection delivers the most value

New textile processing unit

A new dyeing or finishing unit needs reliable steam sized for its machines. A correctly sized fire-tube boiler with proper piping supports production from the first day.

Replacing an old, inefficient boiler

Old boilers often waste fuel. Replacement with a correctly sized unit and good water treatment lowers fuel cost.

Thermic fluid for high-temperature processes

Stenters and some chemical processes need high temperatures without high pressure; thermic fluid heaters serve them.

Why it matters for plants in Pakistan

Local context

Gas curtailment in winter and rising fuel prices push many Pakistani factories to review fuel choice when they replace a boiler. Selecting the right boiler type, fuel arrangement and feed water system at the start avoids years of high running costs.

Practical tips for the best results

  • Size the boiler on real peak steam demand plus planned expansion, not on the old boiler's rating.
  • Confirm fuel availability and cost (gas, coal, biomass or furnace oil) before choosing the burner and furnace.
  • Plan the boiler room layout for safe access, blow-down and future maintenance.
  • Include feed water treatment in the project scope from day one.
  • Ask for performance testing at commissioning and keep the report.
