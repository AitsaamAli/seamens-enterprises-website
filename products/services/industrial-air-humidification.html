Why humidity matters in a mill

Cotton and other fibres lose strength and flexibility when the air is dry, so yarn breaks more often and static makes fibres cling and fly. Holding relative humidity in the right band for each process keeps breakage and fly down.

Humidification also cools the hall, which helps working conditions in Pakistani summers.

Equipment and water chemistry

Fogging, ultrasonic and evaporative systems each suit different halls. Seamens Enterprises' water treatment chemicals are certified by CONAIR and Luwa-Nederman for use in their industrial air-handling, humidification and climate-control equipment installed across Pakistan.

How Industrial Air Humidification works

Relative humidity controls how much moisture fibres hold. Moist cotton is stronger and more flexible, and humid air carries static away. Each process, from spinning to weaving, has its own ideal humidity band.

Humidification systems add fine water droplets to the air: high-pressure fogging, ultrasonic humidifiers and evaporative pads, controlled by sensors and panels. Water quality matters because poor water clogs nozzles and spreads deposits.

Planning a humidification project

A project starts with measuring current temperature and humidity across the hall through the day, noting where conditions vary most and how many machines are running. From this, the system type, number of nozzles or units, water treatment and control layout are designed. Seamens then installs, commissions and trains your staff so the system holds steady conditions season after season.

What you receive

A designed, installed and commissioned system that holds the target humidity across the hall, with trained operators and a simple maintenance routine.

Why it matters for plants in Pakistan

Dry summer months in Punjab lower humidity in spinning and weaving halls, increasing yarn breaks. Humidification keeps conditions steady.
