Buyer's guide to Mechanical Maintenance Contracts

What a contract covers

A maintenance contract defines a list of assets, such as pumps, fans, gearboxes, compressors, boilers and cooling towers, and sets a schedule of inspections and servicing for each. The scope, response times and reporting are written down, so both your team and Seamens know exactly what is included and how performance will be judged.

Condition monitoring finds faults early

Vibration analysis and thermography detect bearing wear, misalignment, imbalance, loose connections and overheating long before a breakdown. Readings are trended at each visit, and a rising trend triggers planned repair at a convenient time instead of an emergency stop in the middle of a production run.

Planned work costs less than breakdowns

A breakdown brings lost output, overtime, rushed parts and sometimes secondary damage to other components. Planned replacement of a bearing or seal is far cheaper. Over a year, a contract usually moves most maintenance work from the emergency column to the planned column.

Reports your management can use

After each visit you receive a short report showing what was checked, what was found and what is recommended. Over time this becomes an asset history that supports budgeting, spare parts planning and decisions about when to replace rather than repair equipment.

Working alongside your own team

Contracts are designed to support, not replace, in-house maintenance staff. Seamens engineers bring specialised tools and experience, while your team handles daily checks. Many clients use the visits as on-the-job training for their technicians.

Emergency response when it is needed

Even well-maintained plants have unexpected failures. Contract clients receive priority breakdown response, with engineers who already know the equipment and its history, which shortens diagnosis and repair time.
