Buyer's guide to Textile Machinery Parts

Parts matched to your machine

Loom and frame parts must match the make and model exactly. Sharing the machine details and part numbers lets Seamens supply the right part first time for rapier, projectile and air-jet looms, spinning frames and finishing machines.

Wear parts that affect fabric quality

Worn healds, reeds and picker sticks cause fabric faults such as broken ends, reed marks and weak picks. Replacing wear parts on schedule protects fabric quality as well as machine uptime.

Stenter and dryer parts

Chains, clips, pins and bearings on stenters affect width control and fabric marks. Good parts keep finishing ranges running at speed.

Reducing downtime

Imported parts can take weeks. A local source for common parts shortens stops and reduces the stock a mill must hold.

Planning spares by machine count

Mills with many identical machines can plan spares by consumption. Seamens can help build a usage-based spares list.

Quality checked on arrival

Parts are checked for dimensions and finish so they fit and perform as expected.

Healds and reeds

Correct healds and reeds reduce warp abrasion and reed marks.

Picker sticks

Good picker sticks keep shuttle and projectile looms running.

Spinning parts

Frame parts affect yarn evenness and breaks.

Why genuine fit matters

A heald frame, reed or picker stick that is slightly out of specification can cause warp damage, irregular picks or premature wear on neighbouring parts. Supplying parts that match the original dimensions and material protects both fabric quality and the machine. Seamens checks part references and dimensions before supply so the part fits first time on your rapier, projectile or air-jet looms, spinning frames or stenters.
