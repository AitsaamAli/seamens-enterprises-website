Water chemistry decides uptime

In a power plant, feed water quality protects the boiler, cooling water quality protects the condenser and exchangers, and steam purity protects the turbine. Deposits on turbine blades cut output and efficiency.

A coordinated programme across all three keeps the plant running at rated output between planned outages.

How support works

Seamens tests feed, boiler and cooling water, sets the chemical programmes and reviews results on follow-up visits, with Water Safe products for boilers and cooling circuits.

How Captive Power Plant Support works

Captive power plants combine boilers, turbines and cooling systems. Each needs its own water chemistry: pure feed water for the boiler, clean steam for the turbine and controlled cooling water for condensers.

Seamens supports all three with water analysis, Water Safe chemical programmes and follow-up visits, so the plant reaches its planned output between outages.

Buyer's guide to Captive Power Plant Support

Water chemistry is availability

In a captive power plant, boiler tube failures and turbine deposits cause forced outages. Strict feed water and steam purity control is one of the most effective ways to keep the plant available and avoid costly grid purchases.

Feed water treatment

Oxygen scavenging, pH control and hardness control protect boiler tubes. Programmes are set to the boiler pressure and design.

Cooling water programmes

Condensers and engine coolers need scale, corrosion and biological control.

Turbine deposit control

Carryover of silica and salts deposits on turbine blades, reducing output. Monitoring steam purity limits this.

Regular review

Monthly data review keeps programmes on target.

Steam purity

Monitoring silica and conductivity in steam limits turbine deposits.

Condenser performance

Clean condensers maintain vacuum and output.

Outage planning

Inspection during outages guides water treatment.

Routine site support

Support usually includes regular site visits to test feed water, boiler water, steam and cooling water, review dosing and blow-down, inspect chemical stocks and pumps, and discuss trends with the plant chemist or shift engineers. Clear monthly reports make it easy for management to see that water chemistry is under control and to plan any corrective work at the next outage.

Emergency help

When water chemistry upsets occur, fast advice limits damage.

What you receive
