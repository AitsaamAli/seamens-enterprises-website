Buyer's guide to Custom Fabrication

Built to your layout

Standard catalogue equipment does not always fit an existing building, a tight boiler room or an unusual process. Custom fabrication produces tanks, vessels, ducts and supports sized for your space and duty, which can save the cost and delay of modifying the building around off-the-shelf equipment.

Choosing the right material

Mild steel is economical for many structures, ducts and water tanks, while stainless steel suits chemical, food and corrosive duties. The choice depends on the liquid or gas handled, temperature and cleaning needs. Seamens reviews the duty with you and recommends the material and thickness before fabrication starts.

Quality in welding and testing

Good fabrication depends on qualified welders, correct joint preparation and inspection. Welds are inspected and tanks are leak or pressure tested where required, so the finished item is fit for service when it arrives on site.

Ventilation and exhaust systems

Hot, humid or dusty production halls need effective exhaust. Seamens fabricates ductwork and supplies industrial belt-drive exhaust fans, sized for the air volume and resistance of the system, to improve working conditions and protect equipment.

Installation included

Fabricated items are delivered and installed by the same team, including lifting, alignment, connections and supports. This removes the gap between a fabricator and an installer and reduces the risk of items not fitting on site.

Repairs and modifications

Fabrication is not only for new items. Worn tanks, damaged ducts and corroded supports can often be repaired or modified, extending their life at a fraction of replacement cost.

Design support

Seamens can prepare drawings from your requirements.

Coatings

From enquiry to installation

A fabrication project starts with your requirement or drawing, followed by a site visit to confirm dimensions, material selection, a quotation with lead time, fabrication and inspection at the workshop, delivery and installation on site. Keeping one team responsible from start to finish avoids fit-up problems.
