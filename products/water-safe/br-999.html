Closed loops are different

In a closed circuit the same water circulates without evaporation, so scale is less of a problem than in an open tower. The main risks are corrosion of steel and copper and the build-up of sludge that blocks coils and strainers.

Because so little water is lost, treatment stays in the loop and protection can be maintained with periodic checks and top-ups.

Checks and top-ups

Seamens sets the BR-999 level from the system volume and water test, then checks the loop on follow-up visits and tops up when needed.

How Water Safe BR-999 works

A closed loop, such as chilled water circulating between a chiller and air handlers, loses very little water. With no evaporation, scaling is minor, but corrosion and sludge can still build up over years if the water is untreated.

Water Safe BR-999 treats closed circuits. Because so little water is lost, a correct treatment level lasts a long time and is maintained with periodic tests and small top-ups.

Buyer's guide to Water Safe BR-999

Why closed loops still need treatment

Closed chilled and hot water loops lose little water, but the water inside stays in contact with steel, copper and other metals for years. Without treatment, corrosion produces sludge, blocks strainers and leads to leaks.

Treatment levels

Treatment level is tested and topped up after any water loss.

Finding leaks

High make-up shows leaks that bring in fresh, untreated water.

Sludge and strainers

Clean strainers and clear water indicate a healthy loop.

Mixed metals in loops

Closed loops often contain steel, copper and brass. Treatment protects all of them.

Annual review

A yearly water test and review keeps the loop protected.

Filling new loops

New or drained loops should be flushed and treated at filling to start protected.

Signs a closed loop needs attention

Dark or cloudy water, frequent strainer blockages, rising pump noise, cold spots in fan coils or chilled water units and a sudden increase in make-up all suggest corrosion or leaks. A water test and loop inspection by Seamens identifies the cause and the treatment needed to bring the loop back under control.

Why it matters for plants in Pakistan

Local context

Many large buildings in Pakistan run chilled water loops for years with little attention. Correct closed-circuit treatment protects pipework that is expensive and disruptive to replace.

Practical tips for the best results

  • Test treatment level in the loop regularly.
  • Find and fix leaks that cause make-up.
  • Check for corrosion products in the water.
  • Keep records of top-ups.
  • Review annually.
