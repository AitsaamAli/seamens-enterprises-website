Water Safe BR-555: Oxygen Scavenger for Boiler Feed Water

Water Safe BR-555 is an oxygen scavenger for steam boilers. It reacts with dissolved oxygen in the feed water, which otherwise causes pitting corrosion inside the boiler and feed system.

Why oxygen is removed

Dissolved oxygen is one of the most aggressive causes of boiler corrosion. It attacks steel in small, deep pits that can perforate tubes even when the rest of the metal looks sound.

Mechanical deaeration removes most oxygen, and an oxygen scavenger removes what remains so the boiler water stays protected.

Dosing point and control

The scavenger is usually dosed to the deaerator or feed line so it acts before the water reaches the boiler. Seamens sets the dose from your feed water and checks residuals on follow-up visits.

How Water Safe BR-555 works

Water exposed to air holds dissolved oxygen. Inside a hot boiler, that oxygen attacks steel in concentrated spots, creating deep pits that can perforate tubes. Deaeration removes most oxygen; a chemical scavenger removes the rest.

Water Safe BR-555 is that scavenger. Dosed to the deaerator or feed line, it reacts with residual oxygen before the water enters the boiler, and a small residual is maintained to protect against oxygen ingress.

Buyer's guide to Water Safe BR-555

How oxygen corrodes boilers

Dissolved oxygen attacks steel and forms pits that penetrate tubes.

Residual testing

A small scavenger residual shows oxygen is removed.

Feed tank temperature

Hot feed water holds less oxygen.

Pitting is hard to see

Oxygen pitting often attacks tubes from inside where it cannot be seen until a leak appears. Prevention through scavenging is far cheaper than tube replacement.

Condensate systems

Air leaks in condensate systems bring oxygen back. Finding them improves protection.

Shutdown protection

Idle boilers can corrode quickly; correct lay-up protects them.

Dosing equipment

Continuous dosing pumps give steady protection.

What good oxygen control looks like

A steady scavenger residual in boiler water, low iron in condensate and no fresh pitting at inspection show that oxygen is being controlled effectively.

Why it matters for plants in Pakistan

Local context

Many smaller boiler houses in Pakistan run without full deaeration, which makes dissolved oxygen a common cause of pitting. A chemical oxygen scavenger fills that gap.

Practical tips for the best results

  • Test residual scavenger in boiler water regularly.
  • Keep the deaerator or feed tank at the right temperature.
  • Minimise air leaks in the condensate system.
  • Inspect for pitting during shutdowns.
  • Dose continuously, not in batches.
