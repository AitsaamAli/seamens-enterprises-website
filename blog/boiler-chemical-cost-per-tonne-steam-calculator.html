Boiler Chemical Cost per Tonne of Steam: Free Calculator and Worked Example

A boiler programme is judged on what it costs per tonne of steam, with testing and visits included. Enter your numbers and compare programmes fairly.

Updated October 20267 min read
Key Takeaways
  • Compare boiler chemical quotes as Rs per tonne of steam, not Rs per kg of chemical
  • Include testing and service visits, which can change the ranking
  • Total cost per tonne = chemical cost + service and testing cost
  • Every figure here is an illustrative input, not a Seamens price

Boiler chemical quotes are often compared on price per kilogram, even though a concentrated product and a dilute product are used in very different quantities. The fair comparison is cost per tonne of steam, with testing and service visits counted in. This guide shows the method, a worked example and a free calculator.

Why compare boiler chemicals per tonne of steam?

Cost per tonne of steam puts every programme on the same footing, because it divides what you spend by what the boiler produces. It makes a high-dose, low-price product and a low-dose, high-price product directly comparable, and it shows whether service visits are worth their cost.

How do you calculate chemical cost per tonne of steam?

Chemical cost per tonne of steam equals daily chemical use in kilograms multiplied by price per kilogram, divided by tonnes of steam produced per day. Steam per day equals steam output in tonnes per hour multiplied by operating hours.

Formulas: steam per day = tonne/h x hours; chemical cost per tonne = (kg per day x Rs/kg) / steam per day; service per tonne = monthly service cost / (steam per day x 30).

What does the per-tonne figure not include?

The per-tonne figure covers chemicals and service only. Fuel lost through blowdown, scale on tubes and downtime are separate costs that a good programme reduces, so track them alongside the chemical cost.

  • Blowdown losses: heated water discarded to control TDS. See the boiler blowdown calculator.
  • Feed water quality: higher TDS means more blowdown. See Lahore groundwater TDS and what it means for boilers.
  • Scale and fuel use: deposits on tubes raise fuel consumption.
  • Downtime: an outage costs far more than a month of chemicals.

What should a boiler chemical quote contain so you can calculate this?

A quote should state the daily dose, the product and price, the testing routine, the visit schedule and any response commitment. Without a daily dose, you cannot convert the price into a cost per tonne of steam.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate boiler chemical cost per tonne of steam?
Multiply daily chemical use in kilograms by the price per kilogram, then divide by the tonnes of steam produced per day. For example, 12 kg per day at Rs 400 per kg on 100 tonnes of steam a day is Rs 48 per tonne.
Should service and testing be included in the comparison?
Yes. Divide the monthly service and testing cost by the steam produced in a month and add it to the chemical cost per tonne, because visits and testing are part of what you are buying.
Why is price per kilogram a poor way to compare boiler chemicals?
Concentrated and dilute products are used in different quantities, so price per kilogram does not show the cost of treating the same amount of steam.
What is not included in the cost per tonne of steam?
Blowdown losses, scale-related fuel use and downtime are separate costs that a good programme reduces, so they should be tracked alongside the chemical cost.
