Why Does Bleaching Weaken Fabric?

Bleaching weakens fabric when hydrogen peroxide decomposes uncontrollably, generating free radicals that attack the cellulose chain itself rather than just the coloured impurities the process is meant to remove. This uncontrolled decomposition, most often triggered by trace metal contamination in the process water or on the fabric, causes tensile strength loss and can leave localised thin spots that show up later as holes.

What Does a Peroxide Stabilizer Actually Do?

A peroxide stabilizer chelates trace metal ions like iron and copper that would otherwise catalyse uncontrolled peroxide decomposition, keeping the bleaching reaction steady and predictable instead of an explosive radical cascade. This lets the peroxide do its intended job — oxidising and whitening impurities — without over-attacking the cellulose fibre it's meant to leave intact.

Definition: An oxygen scavenger in boiler chemistry works on a related principle — removing a reactive species before it can do uncontrolled damage — though the chemistry and application differ from a peroxide stabilizer.

How Much Stabilizer Is Needed Per Bleaching Bath?

Water ConditionStabilizer Dose
Soft water, low metal content1–1.5 g/L
Moderate hardness/metal content1.5–2.5 g/L
Hard water, high metal contamination2.5–3 g/L

Typical peroxide stabilizer dosing runs 1–3 g/L depending on water hardness and metal ion contamination level, with harder or more contaminated water needing the higher end of the range. A stabilizer dose that's too low leaves the fabric under-protected even when the peroxide concentration itself is set correctly.

What Happens Without a Stabilizer?

Without a stabilizer, peroxide decomposition runs uncontrolled in the presence of trace metals, causing fabric strength loss, holes, and uneven whiteness from patchy over-bleaching in some areas and under-bleaching in others. The chemical cost saved by skipping a stabilizer is almost always smaller than the cost of the resulting fabric damage and rejected lots.

  • Bath pH — maintain 10.5–11.5 for stable peroxide activity
  • Water source check — test for iron/copper content before setting stabilizer dose
  • Never skip stabilizer to save cost — the risk-reward is heavily against it
