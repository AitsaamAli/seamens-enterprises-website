How Much Antiscalant Is Needed Per Liter of Feed Water?

Antiscalant dosing for RO systems is typically expressed in ppm (parts per million) of feed water, commonly in the range of 2–5 ppm for moderate scaling potential, with higher feed TDS or scaling tendency needing a dose toward the upper end. The exact figure should be confirmed against the antiscalant manufacturer's dosing guide and the specific feed water's scaling indices rather than applied as a flat default.

This worked example scales directly — for a different feed flow rate or feed TDS, substitute the actual values into steps 1–3 and recalculate steps 4 and 5 the same way.

Does Dosing Change With Feed TDS?

Yes — higher feed water TDS generally requires a higher antiscalant dose because more dissolved minerals mean greater scaling potential as the RO system concentrates the reject stream. Lahore's 800–1,500 ppm groundwater TDS range means dosing should be set from an actual feed water test rather than a generic low-TDS assumption that would leave the system underdosed.

Lahore-specific note: At the higher end of Lahore's TDS range (closer to 1,500 ppm), dosing should typically move toward the top of the antiscalant's recommended range, subject to the manufacturer's specific product guidance and a feed water scaling-index calculation.

What Happens If Antiscalant Is Underdosed?

Cost of underdosing: Reduced permeate flow, higher pressure drop, more frequent aggressive membrane cleaning, and shortened membrane life — the lost production and membrane replacement cost typically dwarfs the marginal chemical savings from underdosing.

Underdosed antiscalant allows scale to form on the RO membrane surface, reducing permeate flow, increasing pressure drop, and shortening membrane life through more frequent aggressive cleaning cycles. The cost of underdosing in reduced membrane life and lost production typically far exceeds the marginal chemical cost saved by dosing too low.

How is antiscalant dosing rate calculated?
Antiscalant dosing rate is calculated by multiplying the target ppm dose by the feed water flow rate, converting units consistently, to get the total antiscalant volume or mass needed per unit time. This same calculation scales from a small pilot system to a full production RO plant as long as flow rate and target ppm dose are known accurately.
