What's the Ideal Humidity for Spinning?

The ideal relative humidity for spinning departments is typically 55–65%, which reduces static electricity and fibre breakage while keeping yarn strength consistent. Humidity outside this range increases end-breaks and yarn quality variation on ring frames and other spinning equipment.

What's the Ideal Humidity for Weaving?

The ideal relative humidity for weaving departments is typically 65–75%, higher than spinning because woven fabric formation is more sensitive to yarn brittleness and static under drier conditions. Maintaining this range reduces warp breaks and improves loom efficiency across the shed.

What's the Ideal Humidity for Sizing?

The ideal relative humidity for sizing departments is typically 50–60%, balancing proper size film drying with enough moisture to avoid excessive yarn brittleness before weaving. Humidity too low in sizing can cause size film cracking; too high slows drying and risks sticking on the beam.

What Happens If Humidity Is Too Low in a Textile Shed?

Consequences of low humidity: Static buildup, yarn brittleness, more frequent end-breaks and warp breaks, and increased airborne fibre fly — all of which reduce quality and increase housekeeping burden.

Low humidity in a textile shed increases static electricity buildup, causes yarn and fibre brittleness, and raises the rate of end-breaks and warp breaks across spinning and weaving operations. It also increases airborne fibre fly, which affects both product quality and shed cleanliness — a cascading cost that's easy to underestimate until humidity control is actually fixed.
