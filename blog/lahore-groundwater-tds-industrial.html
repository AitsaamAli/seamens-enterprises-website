Lahore Groundwater TDS: What 800 to 1,500 ppm Means for Your Boiler, Cooling Tower and RO Plant

Industrial plants in Lahore rarely draw the same water. This guide explains what TDS tells you, how it drives blowdown, cycles and RO dosing, and how to test your own water.

By Ghulam Muhammad Bahoo, Head of Digital PresenceUpdated October 20268 min read
Key Takeaways
  • Lahore groundwater commonly runs 800 to 1,500 ppm TDS, but your borewell can differ, so test it
  • Raw feed water at these levels limits a boiler to only about 2 to 4 cycles of concentration at a 3,000 ppm limit
  • Softening or RO feed water changes boiler economics far more than any chemical dose
  • Every figure in this guide is an illustrative worked example, not a Seamens price or a guarantee

Total dissolved solids (TDS) is the first number any water treatment supplier asks for, and it explains more about boiler fuel use, cooling tower water bills and RO membrane life than almost any other single reading. This guide explains what TDS is, what the commonly quoted Lahore range of 800 to 1,500 ppm means in practice, and how to turn a TDS reading into decisions.

What is TDS, and why does it matter for industrial water?

TDS is the total amount of dissolved minerals and salts in water, usually expressed in parts per million (ppm) or milligrams per litre (mg/L). In an industrial plant, TDS matters because it concentrates as water evaporates: steam leaves a boiler and cooling towers evaporate water, but the dissolved solids stay behind and build up.

TDS is a total, not a diagnosis. Two waters with the same TDS can behave differently depending on how much of it is hardness (calcium and magnesium), alkalinity, chloride or silica. That is why a full water analysis, not TDS alone, is used to design a treatment programme.

What TDS does Lahore groundwater have?

Lahore groundwater commonly runs about 800 to 1,500 ppm TDS. This is the working range Seamens Enterprises uses for planning, and it is a typical range, not a guarantee for any single borewell. Depth, location and season all change the reading, so use the table below only to understand what a reading means, then test your own water.

Your measured TDSWhat it usually means for an industrial plant
Below about 300 ppmLow mineral load, such as softened or RO-treated water; boilers can run at high cycles
About 300 to 800 ppmModerate load; scale and blowdown still need active control
About 800 to 1,500 ppmThe commonly quoted Lahore groundwater range; scale risk is high and cycles are limited without pre-treatment
Above about 1,500 ppmHigh load; pre-treatment such as softening or RO is usually needed before the water reaches a boiler

The bands above are planning guides based on how TDS behaves in boilers and towers. They are not regulatory limits. Always confirm limits for your equipment with the manufacturer.

How does feed water TDS affect a steam boiler?

Feed water TDS decides how much water a boiler must blow down to keep boiler water below its TDS limit. The higher the feed TDS, the lower the cycles of concentration, and the more hot water and heat are discarded through blowdown.

Two formulas do the work: cycles of concentration equal the boiler TDS limit divided by the feed water TDS, and blowdown as a share of feed water equals feed TDS divided by the boiler TDS limit.

Raw feed water at Lahore levels would waste a large share of heated water, which is why boilers are normally fed with treated water.

How does make-up water TDS affect a cooling tower?

Make-up water TDS sets how far a cooling tower can concentrate its circulating water before scale becomes a risk. Circulating water TDS is roughly make-up TDS multiplied by the cycles of concentration, so a higher make-up TDS means fewer safe cycles and more blowdown.

How does feed water TDS affect an RO plant?

Higher feed TDS raises the pressure an RO plant needs and increases the scaling potential on the membrane as the reject stream concentrates. A feed analysis, not TDS alone, is used to choose the antiscalant dose and the recovery rate.

Antiscalant doses are typically a few ppm of feed flow. At 4 ppm on a 10 m3/h feed, the arithmetic is 40 g per hour, or 960 g per day. Use our RO antiscalant dosing calculator for your own numbers and confirm the dose with the product guidance and your feed analysis.

How should you test your own water?

Test your own water by taking a clean sample at the point where water enters the plant, measuring TDS with a calibrated meter, and having a full analysis done for hardness, alkalinity, chloride and silica. A single reading from a borewell is a snapshot, so repeat it across seasons.

  1. Collect a clean sample at the actual feed point, not from a tank that has been standing.
  2. Measure TDS or conductivity with a calibrated meter and record the temperature.
  3. Ask for a full analysis of hardness, alkalinity, chloride and silica.
  4. Repeat the test in a different season, because groundwater changes.
  5. Share the results with your water treatment supplier before you accept a dose or a quote.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the TDS of Lahore groundwater?
Lahore groundwater commonly runs about 800 to 1,500 ppm TDS. This is a typical range, not a guarantee for any single borewell, so test your own water before designing a treatment programme.
Is 1,000 ppm TDS good or bad for a boiler?
Raw water at 1,000 ppm TDS is high for boiler feed. With a 3,000 ppm boiler limit it allows only about 3 cycles of concentration, so a large share of heated water would be blown down. Boilers are normally fed with softened or treated water.
How do I calculate boiler blowdown from TDS?
Cycles of concentration equal the boiler TDS limit divided by the feed water TDS, and blowdown as a share of feed water equals feed TDS divided by the boiler TDS limit. Confirm your boiler limit with the manufacturer.
What cycles of concentration should a cooling tower run at?
Industrial cooling towers commonly target about 3 to 5 cycles of concentration. The achievable figure depends on make-up water quality and on how well the programme controls scale and corrosion.
