How Can I Tell If a Chemical Certificate Is Fake?

A chemical certificate should be verifiable directly with the issuing body using a certificate number, and any supplier unwilling or unable to provide this verification path warrants caution. Certificates with vague or unverifiable issuing details are a warning sign worth investigating further before proceeding with an order.

  • Certificate number present and specific — vague or missing numbers are a red flag
  • Issuing body clearly named — and independently verifiable
  • Validity dates current — expired certificates presented as current
  • Supplier willing to facilitate verification — reluctance is itself informative

What Are Signs a Chemical Product Has Been Diluted?

Signs of dilution include reduced performance compared to previous batches from the same supplier at the same dosing rate, unusual product appearance or viscosity, and lab testing showing lower active concentration than specified. Consistent underperformance despite correct dosing is a signal worth investigating with a lab test rather than simply increasing dose to compensate.

Don't just increase dose: If a product suddenly needs a higher dose to achieve the same result it used to, investigate the batch rather than assuming your process changed — dilution is a real and underdiscussed risk in this market.

Should a Supplier Provide a Certificate of Analysis (COA)?

DocumentationWhat It Confirms
ISO certificateSupplier's overall quality management system
Certificate of Analysis (COA)Actual tested specification of the specific batch received

Yes — a reputable chemical supplier should be able to provide a Certificate of Analysis for each batch on request, documenting the actual tested specifications of that specific batch. Refusal or inability to provide a COA is a significant red flag for buyers concerned about product consistency.

What Should I Do If I Suspect Diluted Product?

If diluted product is suspected, requesting independent lab testing of the specific batch and comparing results against the supplier's stated specifications is the most reliable way to confirm the concern. Documenting the issue and raising it directly with the supplier, backed by lab data, is more effective than an unverified complaint — objective data changes the conversation from accusation to fact.
