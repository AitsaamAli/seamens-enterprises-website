Textile Chemical Cost Calculator: Compare Quotes in Rs per kg of Fabric

Price per kilogram does not tell you what a recipe costs. Enter two quotes, your dose and your liquor ratio, and see the cost per kilogram of fabric.

By Ghulam Muhammad Bahoo, Head of Digital PresenceUpdated October 20267 min read
Key Takeaways
  • The real cost of a textile chemical is Rs per kg of fabric, not Rs per kg of chemical
  • Cost per kg of fabric = price per kg / 1000 x dose (g/L) x liquor ratio
  • A product that costs 30% more per kg can cost 22% less per kg of fabric if the dose is lower (worked example below)
  • Every figure here is an illustrative input, not a Seamens price

Two chemical quotes arrive on your desk. One is Rs 500 per kilogram, the other Rs 650. The cheaper one wins, until you ask how much of each is needed for a batch. This guide gives you the method, a worked example and a free calculator so you can compare any two textile chemical quotes on the number that matters: the cost per kilogram of fabric.

Why is price per kilogram a misleading way to compare textile chemicals?

Price per kilogram ignores dose. A product that costs more per kilogram but works at a lower dose in grams per litre can cost less per batch. The dose and the liquor ratio of your machine decide how many grams of chemical every kilogram of fabric actually receives.

How do you calculate textile chemical cost per kg of fabric?

Cost per kilogram of fabric equals the price per kilogram divided by 1,000, multiplied by the dose in grams per litre, multiplied by the liquor ratio. The liquor ratio converts a concentration (g/L) into grams per kilogram of fabric.

Formula: grams per kg fabric = dose (g/L) x liquor ratio; cost per kg fabric = price (Rs/kg) / 1,000 x grams per kg fabric.

How much does the liquor ratio change the cost?

The liquor ratio multiplies the cost directly. The same dose and price cost three times as much per kilogram of fabric at 15:1 as it does at 5:1, which is why machine type matters when comparing a recipe between a jet, a jigger and a beam.

Where should the dose come from?

The dose should come from your own trial on your own fabric and machine, not from a brochure. A lab-dip gives a starting dose, and a bulk trial confirms it. Keep the g/L concentration constant when scaling from lab to production.

  1. Run a lab-dip with your fabric, water and current recipe as the control.
  2. Record the dose that reaches your quality target.
  3. Confirm it in a bulk batch on one machine.
  4. Enter the confirmed dose in the calculator, not the brochure dose.

What else belongs in a fair cost comparison?

  • Reprocessing: a lower-cost recipe that raises reprocessing costs more overall.
  • Technical support: who adjusts the recipe when the fabric or water changes.
  • Pack size and lead time: stock held in Pakistan avoids production stops.
  • Documents: data sheet, safety data sheet and any buyer-required compliance documents.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I calculate the cost of textile chemicals per kg of fabric?
Divide the price per kilogram by 1,000, then multiply by the dose in grams per litre and by the liquor ratio. For example, Rs 500 per kg at 2.0 g/L and a 10:1 liquor ratio gives Rs 10 per kilogram of fabric.
Why is the cheapest chemical not always the cheapest to use?
Price per kilogram ignores dose. A product that costs more per kilogram but needs a lower dose can cost less per kilogram of fabric, so quotes should be compared on cost-in-use.
What is liquor ratio and why does it matter for cost?
Liquor ratio is litres of liquor per kilogram of fabric, such as 10:1. It multiplies the chemical used per kilogram of fabric, so a higher liquor ratio raises the cost of the same g/L dose.
Where should I get the dose to use in the calculation?
Use the dose that reaches your quality target in your own lab-dip and bulk trial, on your own fabric and machine, not a brochure dose.
