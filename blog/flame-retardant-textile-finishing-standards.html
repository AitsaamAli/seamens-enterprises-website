What Standards Apply to Flame Retardant Export Textiles?

The standard depends on destination market and end-use: ISO 15025 covers limited flame spread testing broadly used across Europe, while EN 71-2 applies specifically to toys and children's products regardless of destination. Buyers typically state the required standard directly in their technical specification, so mills should confirm per order — never assume compliance carries over automatically between product categories.

Verify before shipping: Standards and applicability change periodically. Always confirm the current, applicable standard with the buyer's technical file and, where needed, an accredited testing lab before committing a production run.

How Is Flame Retardancy Tested?

Flame retardancy testing typically exposes a fabric sample to a defined flame source for a set duration under controlled lab conditions, then measures afterflame time, char length, and whether flaming or glowing debris drops from the sample. Results are compared against the pass/fail thresholds set out in the applicable standard, such as ISO 15025, and documented in a test report the buyer's compliance team will request.

Test ParameterWhat It Measures
Afterflame timeHow long the fabric continues burning after ignition source removed
Char lengthExtent of fabric damage from the flame exposure
Flaming debrisWhether burning material drops off during the test

Does Flame Retardant Finish Wash Out?

Some FR finishes are durable through many industrial and home wash cycles, while others are semi-durable and lose effectiveness after repeated laundering, depending on the chemistry used. Buyers requiring durable FR performance — workwear, contract furnishing — should specify a wash-durable finish explicitly and request wash-cycle test data as part of the order documentation.

Which Fabrics Need FR Treatment for EU/US Export?

Children's sleepwear, workwear, upholstery and contract furnishing fabrics, and certain home textiles sold into the EU and US commonly require flame retardant treatment plus supporting compliance documentation. Exact requirements vary by product category and destination market, so exporters should always confirm the specific requirement against the buyer's technical specification before finishing rather than treating every fabric type identically.

  • Children's sleepwear — near-universally regulated across EU/US markets
  • Workwear — industry- and buyer-specific FR requirements common
  • Upholstery/contract furnishing — often mandated for hospitality and public-space use
  • General apparel — usually no FR requirement unless buyer specifies
