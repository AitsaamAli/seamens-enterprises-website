What Does Alkaline CIP Cleaner Target?

Alkaline CIP Cleaner removes organic residues — fats, proteins, oils, and biofilm — that build up on process equipment surfaces during production. It's typically run as the routine, first-line clean after every production run, keeping equipment surfaces free of the organic film that would otherwise support bacterial growth and taint the next batch.

Setting the Right Concentration

Concentration should be set from the validated CIP recipe for the specific line and residue load — a stronger dilution isn't automatically better, since overdosing wastes chemical without improving cleaning outcomes once the correct concentration is already dissolving the residue effectively.

Practical schedule: Alkaline CIP Cleaner run after every production run is the standard pattern for food and beverage lines — the exact concentration and contact time should be validated for each specific equipment type and residue load.

How Is CIP Concentration Checked?

Don't set and forget: Dosing systems drift over time — a concentration that was correct at initial setup can silently underdose weeks or months later without routine verification.

CIP concentration is checked using titration or a calibrated conductivity meter, verifying the actual in-process concentration matches the validated cleaning recipe. Concentration checks should be part of routine CIP validation, not just an initial setup step, since dosing systems can drift over time without anyone noticing until cleaning performance visibly declines.

Building a CIP Cycle

  • Set concentration from the validated recipe — matched to the specific line and residue load
  • Verify concentration routinely — titration or conductivity check, not just at setup
  • Document contact time and temperature — concentration alone doesn't guarantee cleaning validation
  • Run after every production run — the standard pattern for food and beverage lines
