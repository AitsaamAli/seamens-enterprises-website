How Long Does Turnkey Plant Erection Take?

Turnkey plant erection timeline varies significantly by plant type and scale, but typically spans several months from foundation work through commissioning and first production run for a mid-sized industrial installation. Larger or more complex plants extend well beyond this range, and site-specific factors can meaningfully affect actual duration — which is why a project-specific timeline estimate is more useful than a generic industry figure.

What Are the Main Phases of Plant Erection?

PhaseWhat Happens
1. Civil & foundationSite preparation, foundation casting and curing
2. Structural & mechanical erectionSteel structure, equipment positioning and mechanical assembly
3. Piping & electrical installationProcess piping, power and control wiring
4. Control systems integrationInstrumentation, automation and safety interlocks
5. Commissioning & performance testingVerification testing, handover documentation

The main phases of plant erection are civil and foundation work, structural and mechanical erection, piping and electrical installation, control systems integration, and commissioning and performance testing before handover. Each phase typically requires sign-off before the next phase begins — skipping this discipline is a common source of costly rework.

What Causes Erection Delays?

Root cause pattern: Most erection delays trace back to a sequencing or coordination gap between trades rather than any single contractor's poor performance — which is exactly why unified project coordination matters.

Common causes of plant erection delays include late-arriving equipment or materials, civil work started before final engineering drawings are confirmed, permit or utility connection delays, and inadequate coordination between civil, mechanical and electrical contractors. Most delays trace back to a sequencing or coordination gap rather than any single trade's poor performance, which is exactly the risk a unified project coordination approach is designed to reduce.

  • Finalise engineering drawings before civil work starts — the single most preventable delay cause
  • Confirm equipment lead times upfront — long-lead items should be ordered early in the project
  • Secure permits and utility connections early — these often run in parallel with construction, not after
  • Use unified project coordination — reduces handoff gaps between civil, mechanical and electrical trades
