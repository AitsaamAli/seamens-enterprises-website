Cooling Tower & RO Chemical Supplier in Pakistan: How to Choose

Scale, algae and membrane fouling are slow problems that show up as expensive ones. These nine checks help you pick a supplier who prevents them.

Updated October 20269 min read
Key Takeaways
  • Cooling towers usually target 3 to 5 cycles of concentration; ask how the supplier plans to reach and hold them
  • RO antiscalant doses are typically 2 to 5 ppm of feed flow, confirmed against your feed analysis
  • Compare quotes as cost per m3 of water treated, with testing and biocide programme included
  • Seamens supplies Water Safe-1020, Water Safe FC-70/90 and Water Safe RO Antiscalant

Cooling towers, chillers and RO plants rarely fail suddenly. Scale builds on fill and tubes, biological growth spreads in the basin, and membranes lose flux a little each week. A reliable chemical supplier is one who measures these trends and acts early. This guide shows what to check before choosing a cooling tower, chiller or RO chemical supplier in Pakistan.

What does a reliable cooling tower and RO chemical supplier do differently?

A reliable supplier measures first, doses to your actual water, and reports trends, instead of delivering drums and leaving. They test make-up water, circulating water and RO feed, set limits in writing, and revisit those limits when your water or load changes.

In Lahore, groundwater commonly runs 800 to 1,500 ppm TDS, so cycles of concentration, scale risk and membrane scaling potential all depend on the analysis, not on a generic dose chart.

How do you check an RO antiscalant dose?

Check an RO antiscalant dose by multiplying the target dose in ppm by the feed flow in m3/h, which gives grams per hour. A 4 ppm dose on a 10 m3/h feed needs 40 g/h, or 960 g per day.

StepExample
Feed flow10 m3/h (10,000 litres/hour)
Target dose4 ppm (4 mg/L)
Antiscalant per hour4 x 10 = 40 g/h
Antiscalant per day40 x 24 = 960 g (about 1 kg)

Use our RO antiscalant dosing calculator to run your own numbers. The right dose depends on feed analysis, recovery and the product, so always confirm it with the manufacturer's guidance.

How should biological control be planned in a cooling tower?

Biological control should be planned as a routine, not a reaction: a defined biocide and algaecide programme, dosing at set intervals, regular microbiological testing and a response plan if counts rise. A programme that only starts when the water turns green is already late.

Ask the supplier how often products are dosed, how results are measured and what happens when a test is out of range. Our guide to Legionella control in Pakistani cooling towers explains why monitoring matters.

When does an RO membrane need cleaning?

An RO membrane needs cleaning when normalised permeate flow falls, normalised pressure drop rises or salt passage increases beyond the trigger agreed for your plant. Many membrane manufacturers advise cleaning when these values change by roughly 10 to 15 percent, so confirm the figure in your membrane manufacturer's guidance.

What should you ask about chiller water treatment?

Ask a supplier how chiller condensers and closed loops will be protected from scale and corrosion, how de-scaling is done, and how the work fits your production schedule. Condenser scale reduces heat transfer and raises power use, so it is worth controlling.

  • Capacity covered: which chiller sizes the supplier has worked with.
  • De-scaling method: online or offline, and the downtime involved.
  • Inhibition: how metals are protected after cleaning.
  • Records: before-and-after readings for approach temperature or pressure.

What mistakes do buyers make when choosing cooling tower and RO chemicals?

The most common mistakes are buying on price per kilogram, skipping the water analysis, running without a testing routine and cleaning membranes only after output has dropped sharply. Each one turns a small, cheap problem into a large, costly one.

  • Price per kilogram only: ignores dose, cycles and make-up water, which decide the real cost.
  • No analysis: a programme designed without water data is a guess.
  • No test routine: without readings, nobody notices drift until damage is done.
  • Late membrane cleaning: waiting for a large flow loss makes cleaning harder and less effective.
  • One programme for every season: summer load, higher evaporation and warmer water change the demand.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose a cooling tower chemical supplier in Pakistan?
Choose a supplier who analyses your make-up and circulating water first, plans a target for cycles of concentration, proposes a scale, corrosion and biocide programme with a testing routine, and quotes in cost per cubic metre of water treated with documents you can verify.
What should an RO antiscalant quote include?
It should include the feed water analysis, the proposed dose (usually 2 to 5 ppm of feed flow), the product name and datasheet, the daily consumption at your feed flow, the cleaning trigger and procedure, and who supports you when pressure drop or flux changes.
How do I check that RO antiscalant dosing is correct?
Multiply the dose in ppm by the feed flow in m3/h to get grams per hour, then compare actual daily consumption with that figure and watch pressure drop and permeate flow. Confirm the dose with the manufacturer guidance and your feed analysis.
What cycles of concentration should a cooling tower target?
Industrial cooling towers typically target 3 to 5 cycles of concentration. The achievable figure depends on your make-up water quality and the scale and corrosion programme, so it should be agreed from analysis.
